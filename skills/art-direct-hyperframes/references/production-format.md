# Choose the production format

Select the format before choosing a visual route or running a timing validator.
A subject such as AI can support any of these formats. Record the selected
format, viewer promise, source material and applicable checks in `DESIGN.md`.

| Format | Main visual material | Review focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operation tutorial | Recorded input, action, result and verification in the actual workspace | Reproducibility and readable causality; use the short-form operation gate when applicable |
| Case analysis | Relevant people, work, delivery and environments, supported by brief source evidence | Business or human conflict, claim attribution, shot variety and narrative rhythm |
| Artwork or product showcase | Approved source artwork and accurate details or comparisons | Image integrity, framing and inspectable results; do not invent a production recording |
| Story or concept film | Characters acting, object changes, consequences and reactions | Actions actually unfold; a camera move over an illustration does not establish character action |

For hybrid videos, name the dominant format and identify bounded demonstration
sections. Check their input, action, result and verification. When the project
adopts a tutorial timing profile, apply it to the genuine tutorial segment with
its local time origin declared. Do not fabricate clicks to fill a tutorial
contract for a case-analysis film. Record the selected checks and review evidence.

## Case analysis

Build scenes around what someone needs, does, pays for, delivers or rejects.
Research tables, pricing pages and worksheets can substantiate a claim briefly;
their administrative structure should not determine the entire film.

Use [footage-editing.md](footage-editing.md) for shot selection and review. A useful
opening shows the subject and stakes; the first five seconds should make the
viewer understand the question being investigated. Treat this as an editorial
target to inspect, not proof of a future retention rate. Support the first
factual claim with a source at the point it is made.

Keep verified facts, attributed self-reports, estimates and unknowns distinct.
Listed prices do not establish sales; revenue does not establish profit; review
samples do not establish a population-wide success rate. Stock footage of people
at work illustrates a process and cannot prove a company's customers, product
output or financial results. Label scene illustrations where confusion is likely.

## Artwork and stories

For a supplied or approved image, preserve aspect ratio, subject relationships,
and the approved work. Crop and reframe deliberately; do not stretch a device or
silently regenerate the artwork. Generated concepts require a clear distinction
from product testing or real footage when the image could be mistaken for either.

When a film promises an action, confirm the source can show that action. Camera
zoom, Ken Burns movement, transitions and cursor movement cannot stand in for a
person acting, an object changing or a tool producing a result. If the selected
production capability cannot create the required movement, record the constraint
and resolve the missing capability before presenting the result as an action film.

## Preserve the user's direction

When the user approves camera work but rejects repetition or rhythm, keep the
approved direction and revise shot content, source intervals, durations and cut
points. Do not change the subject or rewrite the voiceover simply to make footage
collection easier. Record what was approved, what remains open and which version
the feedback concerns.

User feedback overrides a self-assigned aesthetic score. A rejected version leaves
the active delivery queue; archive it as a reference. A new edit needs new review
evidence even when narration, runtime and technical checks remain unchanged.
