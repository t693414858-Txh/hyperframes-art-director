# Direction Framework

Use this framework to turn an abstract mood into implementation-ready art direction. Select deliberately; do not fill every field with defaults.

Start with the manuscript diagnosis and route from [content-style-router.md](content-style-router.md). This framework defines the selected route; it does not replace content-based selection.

## 1. Route Integrity

State:

- Primary route
- Optional supporting route
- Which system owns composition, typography, palette, imagery, and motion
- The bounded role of the supporting route
- Why the nearest alternative was rejected

Do not blend routes by averaging their aesthetics. Preserve one dominant system and use the supporting route for a specific narrative function.

## 2. Project Fingerprint

Read [project-fingerprint.md](project-fingerprint.md). Separate route-level style from project-level recognition.

Define:

- The specific transformation or subject that should remain recognizable without labels
- A semantic-transformation anchor
- A real source-evidence anchor
- At least one additional recurring anchor
- The generic category or nearby project the result could otherwise be confused with

Do not count palette, type choice, cards, presenter placement, subtitles, or logos as sufficient identity.

## 3. Intended Response

Define what the viewer should feel or do after the video.

Prefer:

- Trust the process
- Feel the cost of delay
- Notice the scale of the result
- Understand one difficult idea
- Want to try the workflow immediately

Avoid direction words that do not imply a visual behavior, such as "modern," "premium," or "cool," unless they are qualified.

## 4. Source Material Hierarchy

Rank the visual materials by narrative importance:

1. Primary: footage, photography, UI capture, data, illustration, or typography
2. Supporting: captions, labels, diagrams, masks, or annotations
3. Atmospheric: texture, light, grain, shadow, or restrained decoration

Do not apply a universal image-to-graphics ratio. Choose the balance by format:

- Product demo: UI or footage usually leads
- Documentary or brand story: imagery usually leads
- Data explainer: data and typography may lead
- Kinetic type: typography is the image

## 5. Composition Logic

Choose one primary spatial system:

- Editorial crop: large image fields, edge tension, restrained text
- Modular grid: repeatable alignment with controlled exceptions
- Split field: two unequal zones with a clear relationship
- Layered depth: foreground, subject plane, and atmospheric background
- Typographic field: text establishes scale, direction, and image
- Sequential reveal: the frame changes meaning as information accumulates

Document where the eye lands first and where it travels next.

## 6. Scene Grammar and Variation

Define two or three identity anchors that remain stable:

- Typographic role system
- Palette and material treatment
- Alignment, rule, caption, crop, or depth motif

Then define the available variation axes:

- Scale
- Crop or camera distance
- Alignment
- Density
- Foreground/background depth
- Image-to-type balance
- Evidence format

Change one or two axes at a structural beat while preserving the anchors. Avoid repeating the same spatial archetype for three consecutive scenes unless repetition is the concept. Do not vary every axis at once.

## 7. Typography Voice

Describe physical and cultural character rather than only font categories:

- Compressed and urgent
- Wide and architectural
- Humanist and conversational
- Mechanical and procedural
- Literary and reflective
- Geometric and exact

Assign explicit roles:

- Display
- Supporting copy
- Labels or metadata
- Numbers or data

Limit the system to the minimum number of families and weights needed to establish hierarchy.

## 8. Palette Roles

Define colors by function:

- Canvas
- Primary text
- Secondary text
- Accent
- Signal or warning, when required

Use explicit values in `DESIGN.md`. Prefer a restrained role system over inventing colors per scene.

## 9. Image Treatment

Specify:

- Crop behavior
- Camera distance
- Subject placement
- Contrast and saturation
- Edge softness or sharpness
- Monochrome, duotone, natural color, or selective accent
- Whether images fill, interrupt, or sit behind typography

The treatment should remain recognizable across scenes even when the source material changes.

## 10. Texture and Depth

Use texture only when it reinforces the chosen world:

- Grain for editorial or filmic tactility
- Paper or print artifacts for handmade or archival direction
- Light falloff for cinematic focus
- Hard shadow for graphic separation
- Optical softness for memory or intimacy

Avoid texture that merely fills empty space.

## 11. Motion Character

Describe motion as behavior:

- Decisive
- Weighted
- Suspended
- Elastic
- Mechanical
- Editorial
- Nervous
- Continuous

Connect the behavior to narrative purpose. Defer exact tween and transition implementation to HyperFrames.

## 12. Editorial Rhythm

Define:

- Typical scene duration range
- Where the longest hold occurs
- Where density increases
- Where a contrast beat interrupts the pattern
- Where the climax occurs
- How the ending simplifies

Avoid equal-duration scenes unless repetition itself is the concept.
