# Content-to-Style Router

Use this reference before choosing typography, palette, motion, or layout. The goal is not to label the subject; it is to identify the visual behavior that best serves the manuscript.

Choose the production format with [production-format.md](production-format.md).
Visual route and production format are separate decisions: Evidence-Led
Intelligence can be a footage-led case film, and Precision Product can be a
showcase without a recorded operation. Neither implies full-screen tables or
a fixed palette across episodes.

## 1. Diagnose the Manuscript

Record this profile:

```md
Primary category:
Secondary category:
Communication intent:
Audience:
Viewing context:
Emotional temperature:
Evidence requirement:
Credibility requirement:
Available source material:
Brand or cultural sensitivity:
Route confidence:
```

### Category

Choose the closest primary category:

- Technology, software, AI, hardware, or product workflow
- Culture, history, publishing, arts, or public-interest story
- Business, finance, research, operations, or data
- Science, medicine, engineering, or formal education
- Government, civic service, nonprofit, or institutional communication
- Lifestyle, food, travel, wellness, or human service
- Craft, heritage, regional culture, or archival subject
- Music, fashion, sport, event, youth, or entertainment
- Brand campaign, launch, manifesto, or recruitment

Use a secondary category only when it changes how evidence or emotion should be presented.

### Communication Intent

Intent outweighs the topic label:

- Explain
- Prove
- Persuade
- Launch
- Document
- Celebrate
- Warn
- Teach
- Mobilize
- Reassure

A technology manuscript intended to reassure public-sector users should not automatically receive a glossy product-launch treatment. A cultural event announcement may need kinetic energy rather than archival restraint.

### Evidence and Credibility

Rate the requirement as low, medium, or high.

- High: health, finance, public policy, scientific claims, measurable business outcomes
- Medium: product demonstrations, education, documentary stories, professional services
- Low: mood films, event teasers, expressive brand statements

As credibility rises, prioritize real source material, captions, provenance, legible data, and restrained claims. Reduce decorative abstraction.

## 2. Select a Route

Choose one primary route. Add one supporting route only when the manuscript contains a bounded secondary function. Use approximately an 80/20 relationship; do not switch full identities scene by scene.

Before selecting, compare the two or three plausible routes. Score each from `0` to `2` on:

- Intent fit
- Source-material fit
- Credibility fit
- Audience and platform fit
- Brand and cultural fit

Use the highest total unless a documented strategic reason outweighs it. A tie means confidence is low or medium, not that both routes should become full visual systems.

### Precision Product

Best for: technology launches, software workflows, hardware, AI tools, premium product demonstrations.

- Intended response: confidence, desire, clarity, control
- Primary material: product UI, device detail, workflow proof, clean typography
- Composition: generous light or neutral space, exact alignment, decisive scale shifts, controlled macro crops
- Typography: neutral or humanist sans with precise hierarchy
- Palette: white, soft gray, near-black, and one product-derived accent; dark mode only when the product or story requires it
- Image treatment: crisp, optically clean, minimal decoration
- Motion: restrained, smooth, purposeful, with long enough holds to inspect the product
- Avoid: generic blue-purple gradients, excessive glass cards, empty futuristic grids

This route may be described as Apple-inspired product minimalism when the user uses that reference, but translate the reference into properties. Do not copy Apple typography, launch layouts, product silhouettes, slogans, interface, or signature transitions.

### Knowledge Explainer

Best for: tutorials, how-to videos, software education, conceptual explainers, professional training, coding or workflow instruction.

- Intended response: follow, understand, remember, apply
- Primary material: real UI, diagrams, step labels, examples, before-and-after states
- Composition: progressive disclosure, clear numbered stages, one concept per frame, deliberate zoom from context to detail
- Typography: highly legible sans with distinct instruction, code, label, and caption roles
- Palette: light or source-derived neutral base with one semantic accent and restrained state colors
- Image treatment: crisp, direct, and minimally decorative; annotations must point to real evidence
- Motion: stepwise and causal, with pauses after important operations
- Avoid: product-launch mystique, unexplained speed, tiny interface captures, decorative diagrams, constant presenter obstruction

Use this route instead of `Precision Product` when the viewer must learn a process rather than admire or desire the product.

### Editorial Documentary

Best for: culture, history, public-interest stories, publishing, arts, profiles, social narratives.

- Intended response: curiosity, reflection, trust, cultural weight
- Primary material: documentary photography, archival material, quotations, maps, captions
- Composition: large crops, asymmetry, column or folio logic, strong image-text tension
- Typography: literary serif for voice plus restrained sans for captions and metadata
- Palette: paper white, ink black, muted photographic color, one editorial signal color
- Image treatment: natural or restrained color, visible captioning, occasional grain or print tactility
- Motion: editorial cuts, measured reveals, uneven scene durations, stillness around important images
- Avoid: fake newspaper pages, ornamental nostalgia, dense article-like body copy

This route may be described as New York Times-inspired editorial rigor when the user uses that reference, but do not reproduce its masthead, type system, page grid, visual identity, or branded graphics.

### Evidence-Led Intelligence

Best for: business, finance, research, operations, dashboards, measurable outcomes.

- Intended response: understand, believe, decide
- Primary material: data, UI, source documents, before-and-after evidence
- Composition: evidence-led crops, annotated comparisons, selective modular alignment
- Typography: clear sans with strong numeric roles and compact metadata
- Palette: neutral base with semantic colors tied to the data
- Image treatment: sharp and legible; decoration remains subordinate to proof
- Motion: sequential explanation, comparison, accumulation, and a held result reveal
- Avoid: dashboard wallpaper, unexplained metrics, futuristic styling that weakens credibility

### Scientific Clarity

Best for: science, medicine, engineering, technical education, complex systems.

- Intended response: comprehend, trust, remember
- Primary material: diagrams, specimens, process imagery, equations, measurements
- Composition: progressive disclosure, controlled layering, explicit labels and scale
- Typography: highly legible sans with disciplined notation and caption roles
- Palette: light neutral field, restrained cool or natural accents, semantic warnings
- Image treatment: precise, high-resolution, minimally stylized
- Motion: causal sequences and spatial explanations; no movement without explanatory value
- Avoid: decorative particles presented as science, ambiguous scale, cinematic effects that obscure evidence

### Civic Trust

Best for: government, public service, nonprofit, institutional information, safety guidance.

- Intended response: reassurance, comprehension, legitimacy, action
- Primary material: people, places, official evidence, clear instructions
- Composition: accessible hierarchy, stable framing, human examples, visible calls to action
- Typography: plainspoken and highly legible
- Palette: light, calm, accessible, and brand-compatible
- Image treatment: authentic and representative rather than staged luxury imagery
- Motion: calm, direct, and predictable
- Avoid: luxury cues, aggressive hype, opaque abstraction, small disclaimers

### Human Warmth

Best for: lifestyle, food, travel, wellness, hospitality, personal service.

- Intended response: intimacy, appetite, comfort, belonging
- Primary material: people, environments, tactile close-ups, lived moments
- Composition: observational crops, breathing room, foreground depth, imperfect human detail
- Typography: humanist, warm, or conversational
- Palette: source-derived natural color with warm neutrals
- Image treatment: tactile, softly contrasty, natural, lightly imperfect
- Motion: patient camera-like movement, sensory detail, held expressions
- Avoid: sterile product grids, over-polished luxury treatment, synthetic lifestyle imagery

### Craft and Heritage

Best for: traditional craft, regional culture, historical process, architecture, material heritage.

- Intended response: respect, continuity, tactile attention
- Primary material: hands, tools, materials, archival evidence, place
- Composition: detail-to-context progression, irregular rhythm, restrained text
- Typography: culturally appropriate serif or vernacular-influenced display face paired with a neutral support face
- Palette: material-derived earth, mineral, ink, wood, textile, or ceramic tones
- Image treatment: texture and age may remain visible; do not manufacture false antiquity
- Motion: weighted, patient, process-led
- Avoid: theme-park folklore, indiscriminate sepia, decorative symbols without context

### Kinetic Culture

Best for: music, fashion, sport, events, youth culture, high-energy announcements.

- Intended response: anticipation, identity, participation
- Primary material: people, performance, bold type, event facts
- Composition: scale collisions, directional type, cropping, controlled density
- Typography: expressive display voice with a disciplined information layer
- Palette: high contrast and culturally specific accents
- Image treatment: flash, print, poster, stage, street, or broadcast language chosen from the subject
- Motion: rhythmic, cut-led, syncopated, with strategic still frames
- Avoid: constant maximum energy, unreadable information, unrelated trend sampling

### Brand Manifesto

Best for: mission films, recruitment, campaign statements, category creation, emotional launches.

- Intended response: belief, aspiration, alignment
- Primary material: one central claim supported by people, product, or consequence
- Composition: iconic hero frames, deliberate escalation, simplified resolution
- Typography: distinctive but limited, with memorable statement scale
- Palette: brand-led and emotionally controlled
- Image treatment: cinematic or graphic, but consistent with the evidence available
- Motion: escalating rhythm and one earned climax
- Avoid: unsupported grand claims, stock-image montage, a climax in every scene

## 3. Resolve Mixed Content

Use these rules in order:

1. Let communication intent choose the primary route.
2. Let credibility requirements control how much evidence must remain visible.
3. Let source material constrain the route; do not promise an image-led film with no usable imagery.
4. Let audience and platform adjust density, pacing, and accessibility.
5. Use a supporting route only for a bounded function such as an evidence chapter, archival prologue, or event climax.

Examples:

- AI product plus public-sector case study: `Civic Trust` primary, `Precision Product` supporting
- Software tutorial plus product announcement: `Knowledge Explainer` primary, `Precision Product` supporting
- Museum technology exhibition: `Editorial Documentary` primary, `Precision Product` supporting
- Financial research launch: `Evidence-Led Intelligence` primary, `Brand Manifesto` supporting
- Heritage festival teaser: `Craft and Heritage` primary, `Kinetic Culture` supporting

## 4. Confidence and Exceptions

- High confidence: one route clearly satisfies intent, evidence, audience, and assets
- Medium confidence: one route leads, but a supporting route materially improves one chapter
- Low confidence: two routes remain plausible because brand position or audience is unresolved

At low confidence, present two short route summaries, recommend one, and state the assumption behind the recommendation. Do not create three cosmetic variations of the same route.

Break a route rule only when the exception improves narrative meaning. Record the exception in `DESIGN.md`.
