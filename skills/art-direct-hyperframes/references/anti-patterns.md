# Aesthetic Anti-Patterns

Use these as diagnostic signals, not universal bans. An intentional exception is valid when it clearly serves the content and is documented in `DESIGN.md`.

## Template-Like Composition

### Repeated Centered Stack

Signal: Every scene uses a centered headline, subtitle, and button-like label.

Risk: The video has no evolving eye path or editorial tension.

Correction: Change the spatial logic between structural beats while preserving one alignment or motif for continuity.

### Equal Card Grid

Signal: Information is placed in identical rounded rectangles regardless of importance.

Risk: Hierarchy collapses and the frame reads as a dashboard screenshot.

Correction: Let the most important item break scale, crop, alignment, or containment.

### Web Page Framing

Signal: Small type, generous page margins, stacked sections, and interface-like spacing dominate the frame.

Risk: The composition does not use the scale or edge tension of video.

Correction: Increase visual scale, use deliberate crops, and establish a clear first and second focal point.

### Persistent Interface Shell

Signal: A large title area, metric row, navigation panel, or dashboard frame remains visible through nearly every scene.

Risk: Individual scenes cannot own the frame, useful evidence is compressed, and the contact sheet looks like one template with changing copy.

Correction: Use the full shell to establish the world, then collapse it to one or two identity anchors such as a chapter rail or progress line.

## Generic Style

### Category Costume

Signal: Technology always becomes dark neon, culture always becomes sepia paper, and finance always becomes blue dashboards.

Risk: Subject labels replace interpretation, so the visual language ignores intent, audience, evidence, and emotional tone.

Correction: Diagnose the communication purpose first, then choose a route using the content-to-style router.

### Prestige Brand Mimicry

Signal: A request for an Apple-like or newspaper-like result is answered by copying a recognizable type system, layout, masthead, product framing, or signature transition.

Risk: The result becomes derivative, may misuse protected brand assets, and fails to build a content-specific identity.

Correction: Translate the reference into properties such as optical clarity, editorial rigor, light neutral space, caption logic, or restrained motion. Build an original system from those properties.

### Default Dark Technology

Signal: A technology subject receives a near-black canvas, cyan glow, glass panels, and particles without evidence that darkness serves the product or viewing context.

Risk: Readability, product color, and credibility are reduced while the result resembles generic AI promotion.

Correction: Start with light or source-derived neutrals for clarity. Use a dark field only when the product, brand, or narrative contrast justifies it.

### Uncontrolled Hybrid

Signal: Every chapter adopts a complete new style because the manuscript contains several topics.

Risk: The video feels like a reel of unrelated templates and loses brand memory.

Correction: Select one primary route for the full system and at most one supporting route for a bounded narrative function.

### Consistent but Monotonous

Signal: Palette and typography are coherent, but three or more consecutive scenes repeat the same crop, alignment, density, and eye path.

Risk: Visual identity is preserved at the cost of attention and narrative escalation.

Correction: Keep two or three identity anchors while changing one or two structural axes such as scale, crop, density, depth, or image-to-type balance.

### Route Style Mistaken for Project Identity

Signal: A light canvas, navy text, colored cards, editorial rules, clean sans typography, presenter corner, and subtitle bar are treated as the project's identity.

Risk: The frames are coherent but can be reused for unrelated meeting, finance, education, or productivity content by changing only the copy.

Correction: Define a content-specific semantic transformation, show real source evidence, and repeat one project mechanism across setup, development, and result. Test recognition after ignoring titles, logos, captions, and presenter.

### Presenter as Identity Substitute

Signal: The same presenter or avatar appears in every frame while the central compositions remain generic.

Risk: The presenter provides channel continuity but does not explain what makes this project or transformation unique.

Correction: Treat the presenter as a supporting focal point. Build project identity into the evidence, transformation, and composition behind them.

### Default AI Gradient

Signal: Blue-purple glow, cyan accents, and dark background appear without content-specific reasoning.

Risk: The result becomes interchangeable with unrelated AI product videos.

Correction: Derive palette and material treatment from the subject, brand, or emotional thesis.

### Decorative Noise

Signal: Floating particles, glows, rings, or grid lines are added only because the frame feels empty.

Risk: Decoration competes with the message and hides a weak composition.

Correction: Fix hierarchy and crop first. Keep only elements that guide attention, establish depth, or reinforce the visual world.

### Style Sampling

Signal: Each scene uses a different visual trend or transition.

Risk: Variety replaces identity.

Correction: Use one composition system, one material treatment, and one transition language, then reserve exceptions for structural changes.

### Claim Without Evidence

Signal: Generic cards, placeholder windows, or category labels stand in for a claim about real files, browser context, data, or outputs.

Risk: The viewer understands the category but does not believe the transformation.

Correction: Replace at least one generic representation in each important beat with real or faithfully derived workflow evidence.

## Typography Problems

### Category-Default Type

Signal: A generic geometric sans is chosen only because the subject is technology.

Risk: Typography contributes no cultural or emotional voice.

Correction: Describe the intended physical character first, then select the family and weights.

### Caption-Shaped Everything

Signal: Headlines, labels, numbers, and captions share similar size and treatment.

Risk: The viewer cannot distinguish narrative hierarchy.

Correction: Assign explicit typographic roles and create meaningful scale and weight contrast.

### Accidental Line Breaks

Signal: Short final lines, collisions, or inconsistent wrapping appear across similar scenes.

Risk: The frame looks uncomposed even when the underlying layout is valid.

Correction: Set intentional measure and size constraints, then review representative rendered frames.

## Motion Problems

### Uniform Entrance

Signal: Every element fades upward with the same duration and easing.

Risk: Motion stops communicating hierarchy.

Correction: Define which element leads, which supports, and where stillness should remain. Let HyperFrames implement compliant variation.

### Constant Ambient Motion

Signal: Every background and object continuously floats, pulses, or zooms.

Risk: Nothing feels important because nothing is allowed to settle.

Correction: Use ambient motion only when it provides depth or sustained attention. Preserve static holds.

### Equal Scene Duration

Signal: Every scene receives the same number of seconds.

Risk: Narrative importance is flattened.

Correction: Allocate time according to comprehension, emphasis, contrast, and climax.

### Sparse Post-Cut State

Signal: A new scene appears with only a headline or one partial panel, then spends too long assembling the information needed to understand it.

Risk: The transition feels unfinished and the viewer briefly loses confidence in the composition.

Correction: Establish the dominant visual and main claim immediately after the transition, then layer supporting detail according to hierarchy.

### Transition Showcase

Signal: Transitions are more memorable than the scenes they connect.

Risk: Technique interrupts continuity.

Correction: Choose one primary transition language and reserve stronger exceptions for real structural changes.

## Presenter Integration

### Floating Obstruction

Signal: A presenter or avatar remains in a fixed corner while essential panels, labels, or evidence extend underneath it.

Risk: The presenter competes with the proof and makes otherwise valid layouts feel crowded.

Correction: Reserve the presenter-safe zone before composing each hero frame. Treat the presenter as a secondary focal point and relocate or crop evidence around that zone.

## Review Failure

### Isolated-Frame Approval

Signal: A few attractive frames are approved without comparing every scene in sequence.

Risk: Repeated layouts, flat escalation, and visual monotony remain hidden.

Correction: Assemble one hero frame per scene into a contact sheet, then inspect the full arc at thumbnail size.

### Technical Pass Equals Aesthetic Pass

Signal: Clean lint, contrast, and overflow reports are treated as proof of visual quality.

Risk: A technically valid video may still be generic, incoherent, or poorly paced.

Correction: Review hero frames and draft playback with the aesthetic scorecard after technical validation.
