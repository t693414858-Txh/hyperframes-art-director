# Project Fingerprint

A visual route controls tone and craft. It does not make a video recognizable as a specific project. Use the recognition and template-swap questions when generic styling hides the subject.

The anchor schema, scene fields and numeric coverage below belong to the optional detailed direction/scorecard profile. Compact review can record the visible subject, distinguishing evidence and any necessary repairs in existing notes. Do not require a story or artwork to become a software transformation to satisfy this schema.

## Recognition Target

Complete this sentence:

`Even with the project name, logo, captions, and presenter hidden, the viewer should recognize this as [specific project transformation or subject].`

Weak targets describe a category:

- A clean technology tutorial
- A light editorial explainer
- A productivity video

Strong targets describe a project-specific transformation:

- A Codex workflow turning a written brief into a verified code change
- A meeting transcript becoming accountable tasks with owner, deadline, and acceptance criteria
- A museum archive becoming an evidence-linked public timeline

## Required Anchor Types

Define at least three recurring anchors. They must include:

1. `semantic_transformation`: the content-specific before-to-after behavior
2. `source_evidence`: real UI, footage, documents, data, or artifacts that prove the project
3. One additional structural, typographic, motion, brand, or material anchor

Supported types:

- `semantic_transformation`
- `source_evidence`
- `structural`
- `typographic`
- `motion_behavior`
- `brand_asset`
- `material`
- `color`

Color and typography alone cannot form a project fingerprint.

## Scene Recurrence

Map fingerprint anchors to scenes. An anchor should recur often enough to become memorable without forcing the same layout everywhere.

- The semantic transformation should appear at the setup, development, and result.
- Source evidence should appear wherever the narrative makes a factual or product claim.
- Structural and motion anchors may recur in altered scale, crop, or density.
- A presenter, subtitle bar, logo, or repeated shell is supporting continuity, not sufficient identity.

For each scene, record:

- `identity_role`: `complete`, `establish`, `develop`, `prove`, `resolve`, or `support`
- `core_identity_scene`: whether the scene carries the project fingerprint
- `template_swap_result`: `requires_redesign` or `copy_only`
- `proof_asset_ids`: production evidence visible in the scene
- `replacement_dimensions`: what must change for another project
- `project_specific_evidence`: what the viewer can actually see

For multi-scene videos:

- At least 60 percent of scenes must be core identity scenes.
- No more than 25 percent may survive with copy-only changes.
- Core scenes must include both semantic-transformation and source-evidence anchors.
- Core scenes must require redesign in at least two dimensions.
- Establish, prove, and resolve identity roles are required for three or more scenes.

A single-scene video uses the `complete` identity role and may set anchor recurrence to one.

## Template-Swap Test

Choose a plausible unrelated project and attempt to reuse the frames by changing only text, logo, colors, and presenter.

Pass only when at least three areas require redesign, such as:

- `composition`
- `source_evidence`
- `semantic_transformation`
- `motion_behavior`
- `data_structure`
- `material_treatment`

If the alternative project works after copy changes, the current system is a template rather than a project fingerprint.

## Blind Recognition Test

Review the contact sheet while mentally or physically ignoring:

- Project name and logo
- Presenter or avatar
- Captions and call to action
- Generic route styling such as light canvas, dark navy, serif/sans pairing, cards, rules, or gradients

Ask:

1. What specific project or transformation remains visible?
2. Which other unrelated projects could use these same frames unchanged?
3. Does at least one memorable project-specific mechanism recur across the sequence?
4. How many frames require redesign rather than copy changes for another project?

Fail when the answer is only a broad category such as meeting productivity, AI tool, business report, or editorial explainer.

## Example: Meeting Notes to Accountable Actions

Generic treatment:

- Warm-white report pages
- Navy headings
- Colored task cards
- Presenter in the lower corner
- Final keyword call to action

This is coherent but interchangeable.

Project fingerprint:

- Raw conversation fragments visibly collapse into one four-field action contract
- Owner, deadline, and acceptance-criteria gaps remain marked until evidence fills them
- Real Codex input or output proves the transformation rather than generic cards
- A recurring `unresolved -> specified -> verified` state change drives composition and motion

The light palette may remain, but it becomes a container for a recognizable mechanism rather than the identity itself.
