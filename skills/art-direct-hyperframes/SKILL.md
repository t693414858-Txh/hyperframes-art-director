# Art Direct HyperFrames

Turn the viewer promise into visible action, understandable evidence and deliberate
editing. Keep decisions specific enough to produce and check.

## Scope the work

| Request | Work to do |
| --- | --- |
| New video or major redesign | Lock the subject and format, establish direction, inspect a representative sample, produce and review |
| Revision | Locate the exact version and failure, preserve approved choices, fix the cause and check affected dependencies |
| Review only | Inspect the available evidence and report timestamped findings; do not edit |
| Narrow polish | Make the requested change and check its effect; reuse the approved direction |

Read the brief, current script, assets, exact active export and existing `DESIGN.md`.
For a series, read current `STYLE-PREFERENCES.md` and the relevant prior episode.
Resolve conflicts using the latest explicit user request and confirmed feedback;
keep old preferences as history rather than applying contradictory defaults.

Record the viewer promise, production format, approved choices, open defects and
success conditions in existing project notes. Preserve the user's topic when
repairing an unclear video. Do not change the topic to suit an easier recording.

Use the configured writing skill for script work, including Sepia where selected.
Follow installed HyperFrames skills for composition, animation, audio and CLI
mechanics. This Skill owns direction and review, not a second implementation API.

## 1. Choose the format and visual direction

Read [references/production-format.md](references/production-format.md). Select
operation tutorial, case analysis, artwork/product showcase or story/concept film.
Identify genuine demonstration sections in hybrid work.

Use [references/content-style-router.md](references/content-style-router.md) when
choosing or changing a visual language. Match purpose, audience and source material.
Named styles such as Apple or editorial newspapers describe transferable visual
properties; they do not determine every technology or cultural video's look.

When adapting a supplied reference, read
[references/reference-analysis.md](references/reference-analysis.md). Inspect the
actual source and map the requested narrative mechanism, shot functions and proof.
Verify named tools and capabilities before writing claims. Label inaccessible
references and inferred matches honestly.

Define what identifies this specific subject through source details and changes
on screen. Use [references/project-fingerprint.md](references/project-fingerprint.md)
when interchangeable styling is the problem; its quantitative scoring profile is
optional unless the project adopts it.

Compare materially different directions when the choice is unresolved. Preserve
an approved direction without manufacturing extra alternatives. For a formal
comparison, use [references/visual-benchmarks.md](references/visual-benchmarks.md).

## 2. Prove the visual approach before scaling

Check source availability, legibility, permitted use and the claims each asset can
support. Use [references/asset-readiness.md](references/asset-readiness.md) when
asset gaps or rights affect the direction.

For a new direction or a repeatedly rejected result, inspect a representative
short sample before producing the whole film. Include the most uncertain action,
an important detail and a transition where relevant. A still artwork needs a
finished image review; a promise of motion needs a moving sample. Reuse existing
audio or a timing guide for a provisional sample without splicing it into the
final approved narration.

Check at phone size: can the viewer identify the point, see the actual change and
read the proof? If the sample fails, fix the material or shot design before more
voice generation and full renders. Keep valid source work and approvals.

When an aesthetic skill is specified, load and apply its actual rules before
recording. Preserve its medium: sparse artwork can be right even when the video
needs comparison, detail or explanation. Do not fill the artwork with decoration
to satisfy a video-density target.

## 3. Plan shots and rhythm

Use the existing storyboard or a compact table: time/beat, visible action or claim,
source, framing, change and cut reason. Each shot should orient, demonstrate,
compare, explain, reveal or resolve something. A deliberate observation hold is
valid. Cursor travel, subtitle changes and decorative zooms do not create proof.

For footage-led films, read [references/footage-editing.md](references/footage-editing.md).
Track source intervals, work stages, actions, environments and shot scale. Compare
repeated source ranges, not exported crop filenames. Keep intentional callbacks
and useful series continuity; replace accidental repetition.

For software tutorials, preserve the causal link between input, operation and
visible result. For case analysis, let relevant people, work and consequences
carry the story with source inserts where needed. For stories, verify that actions
actually unfold; camera movement over a still image cannot substitute for them.

Establish one visual system and vary focus, scale and density within it. Hold long
enough to read important evidence. Change pace at actions and turns in the argument;
do not solve a weak sequence with uniform fast cuts or a new transition per shot.

Adopt timing and interaction targets only for the applicable format and project.
Use [references/review-workflow.md](references/review-workflow.md) to select checks.
Do not add clicks, padding, CTA segments or invented evidence to satisfy a ratio.

## 4. Implement and revise the cause

Hand the selected direction, shot plan, source material and applicable checks to
the installed HyperFrames workflow. Maintain exact colors, typography and motion
decisions in the project's design document.

On revision, record: observed failure and timestamp, likely cause, targeted edit,
affected artifacts and the evidence needed to close it. Distinguish wrong content,
insufficient material, poor framing, editorial timing and audio defects. Fix the
layer responsible; retain unrelated approved choices.

Read [references/final-delivery-review.md](references/final-delivery-review.md) for
narration and export checks. Keep the approved single-speaker voice continuous.
Retiming audio invalidates dependent captions and scene timing; replacing shots
invalidates their visual review. Regenerate spoken content only when it needs
repair, not because a visual revision occurred. Resolve missing required capabilities
within existing authorization; do not silently change the agreed voice or quality.

## 5. Review the actual delivery

Inspect the exact current export and record what was reviewed. Cover these checks
in an existing review note; a new JSON file is not required for every change:

- The intended subject and viewer promise are clear, with truthful evidence.
- The opening, key action, result and ending read at phone size; fonts, captions,
  source proportions and foreground subjects are intact.
- Playback supports the narrative with understandable actions, varied pace and
  no accidental repetition or distracting transitions.
- Narration is complete and continuous; captions and cuts match final timing.
- Export path/version and review artifacts agree; requested defects are resolved.

Capture frame zero and a frame inside the ending, plus decisive moments. Use
[references/review-capture-plan.md](references/review-capture-plan.md) for automated
frame extraction and [references/contact-sheet-review.md](references/contact-sheet-review.md)
for sequence comparison. Still frames alone cannot establish motion or sound quality.
State missing playback or listening evidence rather than claiming those checks passed.

For repeated defects, formal scored review or an existing automated pipeline, use
the detailed contracts in [references/review-workflow.md](references/review-workflow.md).
Technical validation is necessary where applicable; a passing script does not
overrule visible defects or user feedback. Do not reuse an old score for a new cut.

Deliver the active export, concise changes, checks actually completed and unresolved
issues. Distinguish approval of a direction, artwork, rhythm and final publication.
Read [references/recurring-account-automation.md](references/recurring-account-automation.md)
only for an authorized recurring-account delivery or publishing task with a project
contract. Keep publishing mechanics out of ordinary editing decisions.

## Learn without accumulating rules

Use [references/style-preferences-template.md](references/style-preferences-template.md)
to record confirmed feedback with context and the version concerned. Update or
supersede the nearest existing rule before adding one. A recurring defect may
justify a reusable check; a single engine workaround belongs in its project profile.

Keep aesthetic warnings in [references/anti-patterns.md](references/anti-patterns.md)
as diagnostic prompts, not universal bans on cards, dark palettes, stillness or reuse.
Use the named issue to choose a repair and verify it in the next export.
